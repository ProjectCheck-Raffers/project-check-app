# Lessons for the regs checker (from the job library)

These come from real jobs. They are evidence of what happened, not legal advice.

1. **No Building Control pass is proven.** None of the jobs has a signed completion or final certificate on file. One job had only blank forms and an unfilled completion template. Project Check should ask users to upload the signed certificate, and must not claim a spec "passed Building Control" without it.
2. **Typical specified U-values (W/m²K).**
   - Standard insulated build: roof 80 mm PIR = 0.27, walls 60 mm PIR = 0.36.
   - Higher spec: 0.21 roof / 0.27 walls.
   - Chill room: 0.19 walls.
   - Unheated store: walls as poor as 0.54 (40 mm PIR).
   - Fire-rated 120 mm mineral wool: 0.40.
   - Unheated buildings relied on a Part L "no heating" statement.
   - Quoted values should be checked against panel thickness (one quote looked wrong).
3. **Boundary distance drives fire walls.** On 3 jobs, the walls near the boundary were 120 mm mineral wool rated for 60 minutes, and the other walls stayed PIR. One wall 7.6 m from the boundary was allowed only 21% unprotected area. Building Control asked for **BR 187 calculations**. Ask for the boundary distance on every elevation.
4. **Building Control full-plans checklist (real request).**
   - Fire strategy (Approved Document B vol 2 or BS 9999): travel distances, exits, emergency lighting and detection.
   - Fire-service proforma.
   - BR 187 calculations.
   - Fire appliance access, with hydrants within 90 m.
   - Tested fire-rated details.
   - Drainage and rainfall calculations (Part H).
   - Part M access and visual contrast.
   - Ground-improvement details.
   - Existing and proposed drawings.
   - Submission through the Principal Designer.
   - Regulation 38 fire safety information.
   This was asked for even on a small extension next to an existing building.
   - **Fill in the fire-service proforma.** One went in blank and the fire service replied "insufficient detail". A short fire strategy (one compartment of about 3,400 m², no sprinklers, 2 exits about 24 m apart), a fire manual, a boundary report (4.11 m) and a drainage calculation then got an "acceptable standard" reply.
5. **Planning routes.**
   - Most jobs went through full planning, including open canopies and a tent hall.
   - One extension used a **Class H Lawful Development Certificate at a 49% floor-area increase**, just under the limit.
   - **Raising the height after permission needed a variation application.**
   - Typical conditions and requests: external lighting details, vibration/piling method statements, noise assessment, vehicle swept paths, biodiversity net gain, and no surface water onto the highway.
6. **Fire exits change.** The number of fire doors changed between quote and build (8 to 3). Base exits on the fire strategy. An inward-opening single door is a question to raise.
7. **Get consents before work starts.** On one job the start date was planned before Building Control approval and before the height variation was granted. On the same job, foundation pads were dug and one was poured before full-plans approval. The inspector warned that this was at the builder's own risk and asked for every pad to be photographed. On another job the order and install slot were booked before the planning decision.
8. **Customer-scope civils cause problems and costs.**
   - Slab strengthening, falls and drainage at the joins caused water ingress.
   - Planning-led attenuation (90 m³, 2 l/s), coal-mining or ground-gas zones, and crane hard standing (about 42 t per outrigger) all added cost or risk.
   - Flag these as "get professional support".
9. **Wales and Scotland are different.** Wales has its own guidance and separate SuDS (SAB) approval. Scotland uses building warrants and the Technical Handbook. Treat both as "need more info or get professional support".
10. **Smoke vents and parapets** were regs-driven extras that weren't always in the supplier price.
11. **Open canopies still need planning, and still raise fire questions** (jobs F and P). An aluminium canopy with a translucent PVC roof was proposed for full planning. An early layout about 2 m from the boundary needs an external fire spread check, and the PVC roof needs its fire classification on file.
12. **Highways and parking can hold up small schemes.** On a 133 m² unit, highways recommended deferral over parking-bay sizes (2.4 × 4.8 m minimum), a lost pedestrian gate, disabled and cycle parking, and EV charging. Moving a door after consent needed a non-material amendment.
13. **Duty-holder roles.** On two builds the client was named Principal Designer and Principal Contractor in the method statement. Check the client understands those CDM duties.
14. **Scope drifts between quote and build.** Doors went from none to 4 roller doors, the length went from 20 m to 17 m, and ridge heights differed between quote and drawings. Re-check doors, fire exits and dimensions against the planning drawings before building.
